You have a plan for the money they leave with. You do not have a plan for the person who walks out.

Australians are retiring at the fastest rate in the nation’s history. Your most experienced people are in that number, and most of them have spent forty years planning for a date and about ten minutes planning who they will be when it arrives.

That gap does not stay with them. It arrives at your desk as a recruitment bill, a slower quarter, a culture reading itself in the car park, and a cohort ten years out quietly deciding what forty years is worth here.

This document sets out the exposure and what closing it involves. It takes about six minutes to read.

2.0

Plate 01

The handover meeting covered passwords, files and the client list. It did not cover the part that actually walked out of the door, and it did not cover what happens to him on the Monday after.

3.0

The exposure

01

Recruitment

You are not backfilling a role. You are backfilling a relationship map, an undocumented process and twenty years of judgement. The advertised salary is the smallest line in that cost. Search, onboarding and the long months before a replacement is genuinely useful are the rest of it, and they are rarely counted against the same budget.

02

Productivity

Work slows in the quarter before a senior exit and stays slow well after it. Decisions queue. Approvals route to people who now have to ask. The handover document, where one exists, records the steps and not the reasoning, and the reasoning was the only part that mattered.

03

Culture

Every remaining employee reads how you retire people. If the exit is a cake, a speech and a car park, they have learnt what forty years is worth in this organisation. If it is a planned and supported transition, they have learnt something else. You are teaching either way, and you do not get to choose whether the lesson lands.

04

Morale

The cohort three to ten years out is watching closely. Some of them will quietly withdraw from the long projects. Some will leave early rather than walk into the ending they have just watched. Neither shows up in an exit interview, because neither of them is given one.

None of the four is a financial failure. Every one of them is a planning failure, and planning is the part you can actually do something about.

4.0

Career track, 1985–2035

One senior employee, plotted end to end. The financial plan is fully specified across the whole span. The psychosocial plan is specified nowhere, and the last third of the track is where that starts to cost you.

Final third of track — unplanned psychosocial exposure

Career track Subject: one senior employee 1985
Scroll to advance the track
  1. 1985

    Intake

    Starts on the floor. A superannuation account is opened in the first week and is not thought about again for thirty years.

  2. 1991

    First promotion

    Given a team. Learns the business by repairing things nobody wrote down.

  3. 1997

    Specialisation

    Becomes the person the others ask. Value starts compounding here. Record-keeping stops here.

  4. 2004

    Custodian

    Holds the client relationships, the supplier history and the reason the process is done that way and not the other way.

  5. 2011

    Senior

    Sits on the decisions. Named in the succession plan. Never once asked about it directly.

  6. 2017

    Financial plan

    Adviser engaged. Contributions modelled to the year. The date becomes a number in a spreadsheet and everyone relaxes.

  7. 2019

    Exposure begins

    The last decade of a working life is when psychosocial preparation should start. Almost nobody starts it here, including your organisation.

  8. 2024

    Withdrawal

    Still entirely competent, quietly stepping back. Declines the projects that finish after the date. This is never logged as a risk.

  9. 2028

    Exit

    Card, cake, speech. Forty-three years of identity finish at four o’clock on a Friday afternoon.

  10. 2030

    The drop

    Direction, identity, purpose and connection go at roughly the same time. The money is fine. The person is not.

  11. 2033

    Consequence

    Health decline, strain at home, a return to work on much worse terms, or a very long quiet. Your organisation hears about none of it.

  12. 2035

    End of record

    Nothing on this track was a financial failure.

5.0

The intervention

What it is
Structured psychosocial education for people approaching the end of a working life. Delivered before the date rather than after it.
What it is not
Financial advice. Dr San does not go near your people’s money. This is the yin to financial planning’s yang, and the two are meant to run together.
Method
human-centred retirement planning™, delivered through readysetretirement psychosocial planning®.
The four exposures
Direction. Identity. Purpose. Connection. Each one named, assessed and planned for separately, because they fail separately.
Working window
Three to ten years out. Six weeks before the farewell is not preparation, it is triage.
Formats
Training. Workshops. Seminars. One-to-one coaching. Group coaching. Consulting. Speaking. Run in-house or off-site, for a cohort or for one person.
Who delivers it
Dr Sandra Walden-Pearson, based in Far North Queensland and working nationally. PhD in business, MBA in human resources, Certified Professional Retirement Coach, accredited workplace mental health first aider.
Evidence of need
Retirement has a recognised dark side: loss of direction, identity, purpose and social connection. The financial side of this transition is well served in Australia. The human side is barely served at all.
Outcome
The mastery still leaves. It leaves on a schedule you set, with the reasoning written down, and the person leaves prepared. You run the exit on purpose instead of by surprise.
