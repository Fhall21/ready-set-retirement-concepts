Financial readiness is one vital sign. It is not the only one.

We spend forty years financially planning for retirement and about ten minutes planning who we will be when it arrives. The balance gets modelled, reviewed and stress-tested every year. The person gets worked out on the drive home from the farewell.

Ready Set Retirement is psychosocial retirement education. It is the yin to financial planning’s yang. Money funds a lifestyle. It cannot buy a quality of life, and it has never once told anyone what to do on a Monday.

Work is not only income. It is structure, status, a diary, a reason to leave the house and roughly half of everybody you speak to in a week. All of that ends on the same afternoon as the salary, and only one of them was on the plan.

The rhythm is regular and nobody has to think about it. Direction, identity, purpose and connection all arrive with the job, free of charge, every weekday, for about forty years.

The trace is a drawing, not data. It is here because the shape of the thing is what people recognise, and the flat part is the part nobody was warned about.

Deficits D1 – D4

Four things stop arriving on the same day.

Retirement has a recognised dark side, and it is not mysterious. It is four supplies that were bundled with the job, withdrawn at once, without notice, in a week when everyone is congratulating you.

Deficit — unprepared Watch what happens to each of these when the structure that supplied them is removed.

Differential Two plans, one date

A financial plan is excellent at exactly the things it covers.

This is not a criticism of financial planning. It is a description of its scope. Read the right-hand column and note that not one line in it is a money problem, which is precisely why the money plan was never going to reach it.

Intervention Education, applied early

Preparation does not remove the drop. It shortens it.

The four supplies can be rebuilt, deliberately, from other sources. That work is much easier to do three years before the date than six months after it, which is the entire reason this exists as education rather than as repair.

For individuals, the work is usually one-to-one or in a small group, and it starts with the parts of the plan that have nothing to do with money. For organisations facing the Silver Tsunami, the same material runs as workshops and seminars, and it has the useful side effect of making people leave well rather than suddenly.

None of it is financial advice, therapy or treatment. It is education and coaching, delivered by somebody who has watched capable people come apart six months after the farewell cake and decided that was avoidable.
