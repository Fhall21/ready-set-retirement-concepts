We spend forty years planning the money and about ten minutes planning the person. This sheet covers the second part.

Retirement has a well-recognised dark side. Direction, identity, purpose and connection all rest on the working life, and when the working life stops they have nowhere to rest. A financial plan runs right up to the date and then hands you a blank page.

The blank page is the problem. Not the money. Nobody surveyed the ground on your behalf, so the first year gets walked without a map, usually alone, usually by someone who has never failed at anything before.

ST.01 · You are here

Thirty-odd years of fully mapped ground. Every ridge named, every crossing marked, every Monday accounted for. You know what you are called when somebody introduces you at a function.

ST.02 · Limit of survey

The contours thin out somewhere between ten years and ten months before the date. The financial plan runs right up to this line and stops. It was never asked to go further.

Interior not examined

This is what retirement looks like when nobody has been over the ground on your behalf. It is not empty because there is nothing there. It is empty because no one surveyed it.

Haz.01 · Direction

The calendar empties and nothing refills it. Nobody sends the next thing. What reads as freedom at the farewell reads as a very long Tuesday by about June.

Haz.02 · Identity

You were the one who knew how the place actually worked. Now you are described in the past tense by people who are being kind about it.

Haz.03 · Purpose

Work supplied a reason to get up whether or not you liked the work. Very few people have a second reason drafted, and none of us can draft one in the fortnight after the cake.

Haz.04 · Connection

Most work friendships were structural rather than chosen. Take away the structure and count how many are still ringing in August. That count is the one people find hardest to say out loud.

ST.03 · Resurvey begins

None of this means the decision was wrong. It means it was unaccompanied. Psychosocial planning is simply the survey nobody ran: the ground examined before you walk it, not after you are lost on it.

ST.04 · Training and workshops

Run inside organisations losing their most experienced people. Half a day through to a full program. Practical, adult, and aimed at the leaving rather than the leaving party.

ST.05 · Seminars and speaking

For conferences, member bodies and boards. The case for treating retirement as a workforce risk with a date on it, rather than a farewell with a card.

ST.06 · One to one coaching

Private work, three to ten years out, or already in it and finding it sour. Direction, identity, purpose and connection taken one at a time, in the order you can bear them.

ST.07 · Group coaching and consulting

Small cohorts leaving around the same time, and advisory work for the people designing the exit. The route ends at a fixed point: a trig station, plotted below.

Two approaches to the same ground

Approach A · Individuals

For people three to ten years out, and for people already in it and quietly not coping. One to one and small group work on the four things a financial plan does not cover, plus the awkward practical ones: what you say at a barbecue, what your partner has assumed, what happens to the parts of you that only existed at work.

Money funds a lifestyle. It cannot buy the quality of one.

Approach B · Business

Australians are retiring at the fastest rate in the nation's history, and the people going first are the ones who know how the place actually works. That is a recruitment cost, a productivity cost, a culture cost and a morale cost, arriving together.

Training, workshops and advisory work that treat departure as workforce planning rather than an administrative event with a cake attached.

Trigonometrical station

A trig station is a known point. It is not the most interesting thing on a sheet and it is not the destination. It is simply the one position the surveyor is certain of, from which every other bearing on the map is taken.
