Ready Set Retirement

They call it the sunset years.

The sun does not stop. It crosses a line and comes up somewhere else. So does a working life, provided somebody has thought about it first.

Scroll

Forty years of planning for a date. About ten minutes planning for a life.

The money question gets asked early and answered often. The other question, the one about who you will be on the Tuesday after the farewell cake, is left to sort itself out. It rarely does. Four things are attached to your work, and all four leave on the same afternoon.

Direction.

Monday had a shape, and the shape was not yours to negotiate. The first fortnight feels like leave. The sixth month does not.

Identity.

You were introduced by your title for thirty years. Nobody tells you how much of you was sitting inside it until it is gone from the sentence.

Purpose.

Work handed you problems worth solving and people who needed them solved. Nothing arrives to replace that. You have to build it, and building takes longer than anyone expects.

Connection.

Most of those friendships were structural rather than chosen. Take away the structure and you find out quickly which ones were the other kind.

For a lot of people, this is the part nobody planned.

There is a second horizon.

This is not a consolation. It is a different position of the light, and it sits higher than the one you left. Reaching it is work. The work has a method, and the method can be taught.

Education is what moves it.

Ready Set Retirement is psychosocial retirement education. It is not financial advice. It is the yin to financial planning's yang, and it decides whether the money you saved buys a life you recognise.

The work starts three to ten years out, while you still have leverage: time, colleagues, energy, and a title that still opens doors. Preparation is cheap at that stage. Afterwards it becomes repair, and repair costs more.

readysetretirement psychosocial planning® is a structured program. You map what work is actually doing for you. You test what happens when it stops. Then you build the replacements and practise them while you still have somewhere to be on Monday. That is human-centred retirement planning™, and it is teachable.
