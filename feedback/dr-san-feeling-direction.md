# Dr San's feeling direction

For any agent doing design or copy on the site. Source: Dr San's own meeting notes (`feedback/dr-san-meeting-notes.txt`, Oct 2026, file dated 5 Oct 2026). This file records the FEELING she wants customers to have and the ideas she keeps returning to. Where it conflicts with `design-taste.md` or `copy-direction.md`, the resolutions in section 2 apply. Hard rules in those files (no deadline pressure, no SaaS tone, no invented proof) still stand.

## 1. Who she is

Dr San is a teacher at heart (she played "school" at five). She taught high school and TAFE, saw that what happens outside the classroom shapes what happens inside it, and moved into the social sector. Education for social good is her recurring theme: education can make a difference to neglected people, it is not exclusive to anyone, and she is a lifelong learner who then helps. She was an English teacher, so words matter to her and she will notice careless copy. She is the first Australian-based Retirement Coaches Association certified professional retirement coach and the only psychosocial retirement educator. Qualifications: PhD (business), MBA (HR), BA Dip Ed. She created the Freedom Entrepreneur System (FES). She writes her own copy by replacing text in a Word template, so copy should be easy for her to edit and true to her voice.

## 2. The feeling to create

**"Oh my God, I want to talk to her."** That is the reaction she wants on landing. The site's job is to make a visitor want a conversation with this person. It is not to explain a method.

**Relief, and being guided by a real person.** She wants clients to feel she is their saviour. Translate that as warm rescue: someone steady has seen this before, knows the way and will walk it with them. Show it through her face, her plain voice, an easy next step and the sense that the confusing part is about to become manageable.
- Tension: "saviour" invites hype, rescue-from-disaster framing, fear and urgency. `design-taste.md` and `copy-direction.md` ban deadline pressure, salesy copy, gloom and SaaS-style trying too hard.
- Resolution: the rescue is relief, never alarm. Name the problem calmly, once, then show a person who knows what to do. Let the reader feel lighter by the end of each section. Never say or imply "before it's too late". Claim only what is true (credentials, the one real Warren Entsch quote). Her authority carries the weight, so the copy does not need to shout.

**Proactive, by seeing things evaporate.** The Monday/calendar section works because people can see what they will lose if they do nothing: structure, plans, people dropping off the calendar. She says seeing things evaporate is powerful and makes people proactive. Keep that motion. It stays passive (scroll-driven) and quiet, shows the "before" long enough to register, and leaves the reader feeling "I can do something about this", not afraid. Put it in the Monday section, not the hero.

## 3. Core ideas to reuse

- **Finance buys a life, not a life worth living.** Financial planning has dominated retirement. A life worth living meets psychological human needs. Use it inside the argument, as the complement to money planning, never as a hero hook or a dig at financial planners (`copy-direction.md` rules 5 and 7).
- **A bigger reason to get out of bed than the dog's breakfast.** Her signature line. Plain, human and slightly funny. Use sparingly, as one felt moment, not a slogan on every page.
- **The five things work gives and retirement removes:** Structure, Identity, Purpose, Sense of direction, Connection. These are the dials she changes. The work/pause section and the dials table must use exactly these five, in this order and with these names. Update any older labels to match.
- **Control and structure (Freedom Entrepreneur System).** Poor work design means low control. In retirement the structure work gave you goes, and what remains is amorphous. FES gives people back a sense of control. Describe it as getting structure and control back, in plain words.
- **The dark side of retirement.** There are hazards people do not understand until they dip a toe in: no routine, no reason to get up. Say it honestly and calmly, without dark imagery, and always move on to what helps.
- **More choice is not better quality.** More available does not mean better quality of life or relationships. Use only where it clarifies.
- **Education, not exclusive to anyone.** Dr San teaches the why, what, how and when. The tone is that of a teacher who respects the learner: no jargon, no gatekeeping, no "insider" language.

## 4. Type and visual rules

- Against avant-garde type and anything that pulls focus. Use readable, familiar, human faces. No novelty display fonts, no oversized decorative type, no type effects that compete with the words.
- Human-centred throughout: a person (Dr San) before an idea, warm photos, plain layout, calm pacing.
- Keep the "Book a consult" button prominent on every page. She likes it. One clear primary button per view, friendly wording ("Ready to chat?", "friendly chat"), never buried and never competing with a second CTA.
- Her words matter: copy has to read like a good English teacher wrote it. Short, correct, rhythmic sentences. No filler or AI-sounding parallelisms.

## 5. Do / Don't

Do
1. Make the visitor want to talk to her: person, voice and the invitation come first. (Notes, Oct 2026)
2. Give relief: name the problem calmly once, then show a guide who knows the way. (Notes; `copy-direction.md` rule 4)
3. Use the five needs (Structure, Identity, Purpose, Sense of direction, Connection) consistently in dials, copy and FAQ. (Notes)
4. Let seeing things evaporate (Monday/calendar) make people proactive, via passive scroll motion. (Notes; `design-taste.md` section 2, dials)
5. Keep "Book a consult" prominent, friendly and single. (Notes; `copy-direction.md` rule 6)
6. Write plain, correct, teacherly English with one idea per block. (Notes; `copy-direction.md` rule 3)

Don't
1. Don't write "saviour", rescue or fear language literally. No hype, no "before it's too late", no deadline pressure. (Notes tension; `copy-direction.md` rule 4)
2. Don't lead with the money comparison or put the whole argument in the hero. (`copy-direction.md` rules 1 and 5)
3. Don't use avant-garde or attention-grabbing fonts or effects. (Notes; `design-taste.md` anti-patterns)
4. Don't make it look or sound like a SaaS product or a gloomy meditation on ageing. (`design-taste.md` section 1, `rsr-not-saas`)
5. Don't invent proof, stats, credentials or quotes. Warren Entsch is the only testimonial. (`design-taste.md` section 5)
6. Don't use jargon or "psychosocial" outside her title. Education means no gatekeeping. (Notes; `copy-direction.md` rule 7)
