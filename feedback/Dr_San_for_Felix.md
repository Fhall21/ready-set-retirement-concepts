## 2. THE SUNSET YEARS

### Why it catches my eye

This one catches my eye in that I can immediately identify the image/feel of rsr that doesn’t fit.

### Colour

Night sky associates with getting darker as the night (years) progresses – depressing, in this retirement context.

### Mood

‘Sunset Years’ connotation: endings/ageing/passing.

### Phrase

**Excellent:**

Four things are attached to your work, and all four leave on the same afternoon.

The work starts three to ten years out, while you still have leverage: time, colleagues, energy, and a title that still opens doors. Preparation is cheap at that stage. Afterwards it becomes repair, and repair costs more.

readysetretirement psychosocial planning® is a structured program. You map what work is actually doing for you. You test what happens when it stops. Then you build the replacements and practise them while you still have somewhere to be on Monday. That is human-centred retirement planning™, and it is teachable.

### Movement

Great vertical sequence of those things and the development of ‘what it looks like in practice’.

We spend forty years planning for retirement financially and roughly ten minutes planning who we will be when it arrives. The money question gets a professional, a strategy, an annual review and a spreadsheet that runs to age ninety. The other question gets a good luck and a card.

That imbalance is not a character flaw. It is what happens when one half of a problem has an entire industry attached to it and the other half has almost nobody.

Money funds a lifestyle. It cannot buy a quality of life, and it has never once told anyone what to do with a Wednesday.

## 6. VITALS

### Why it catches my eye

This one catches my eye in the positive. The four dials are excellent – simplicity in the visuals; by employer; by individual. So many words captured in this representation, together with the covered/not covered table.

### Colour

Sombre and boring

### Mood

Business-like and not unlike a dashboard reading – assume many will find this layout familiar and so less threatening. Further, the table looks like a health benefits table (dental covered/prosthetics not covered) and so, again, easy to read (it pays to compare lol)

### Phrase

**Excellent:**

Known as Dr San. She works out of Far North Queensland with individuals who are three to ten years from a retirement date, and with the HR and workforce planners who are about to lose thirty years of institutional knowledge in a single quarter.

She is not a financial adviser and has no interest in becoming one. She teaches the other half of the plan, which is the half that decides whether the first half was worth doing. In addition, the adjacent table approach is interesting.

Lastly, Thirty minutes, at no cost, well before the date.

It is a conversation, not a clinical assessment. You describe the date and what you have planned for it. Dr San tells you plainly which parts are covered and which parts are not, and what the second plan would need to contain.

- This is an interesting approach as I had not thought of critiquing plans but the covered/not covered approach works perfectly with it.

### Movement

Excellent for the reasons covered in ‘mood’.
