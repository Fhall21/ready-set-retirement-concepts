# Second round — what your notes changed

San, thank you. That was the most useful kind of feedback, because it was specific and it was
consistent. Read together, your seven notes say two things:

1. **The writing is landing.** You marked something *excellent* on six of the seven, and in one
   case wrote "FELIX – This is so on point!!!!" next to it. Not a single line you marked has been
   changed.
2. **The colour is not.** Six of the seven were marked down on colour and nothing else: *dark and
   dreary*, *depressing*, *monochrome dreary*, *sombre and boring*, *sombre and boring*, *sombre
   and boring*. The paper-like, aged colour got its own mention.

So this is a colour round, plus two rebuilds where you told us the thinking was too hard.

## The one thing we need back from you

Every page now has a small **A / B** control in the bottom-right corner. It changes the colour
and nothing else, so if you prefer one you are telling us about colour, not about layout. Your
choice follows you from page to page, so you can walk the whole set in A and then walk the same
set again in B.

**A — violet and coral.** Warmer, higher energy, more distinctive. The brand purple brightened
and put on white, with coral as the alarm.

**B — orchid and azure.** Cooler and more corporate. Orchid carries the psychosocial, azure
carries the business case. Better suited to the HR and board audience, where violet can read as
soft.

Both are brighter than anything you saw last time, and neither contains a brown, a grey-blue or
an aged cream. Tell us which one you keep reaching for. That is the whole question.

## Rebuilt

### Vitals
You singled out the four dials and the covered/not-covered table, and said the dashboard layout
felt familiar and therefore less threatening. Those are now the entire page.

- The four dials are one bank with a switch: **no plan / with preparation**. Previously there were
  two separate banks of four with eight paragraphs between them, and you had to hold the first
  set in your head to understand the second. Now the change happens in front of you, to the same
  four dials.
- The ECG trace has gone. It was the most complicated thing on the page and it made the dials'
  point forty seconds earlier.
- The table is down from eleven rows to seven, in plainer words: *what a Tuesday looks like*,
  *who you are without the title*, *something to be useful for*, *people who still call*.
- Four disclaimers became one. Eleven credential lines became four, with the rest one click away.
- About two thirds of the words are gone. Nothing you marked as excellent is among them.

### The Map
Your note was the clearest signal in the document: *brilliant, but not a good fit — people may
already come to it stressed, so this needs to be stress free, not a challenge to understand.*

The idea is kept. The surveyor's apparatus is gone: no grid references, no elevation, no
bearings, no scale bar, no north arrow, no ten-item legend, no fold lines, no paper grain. The
words *traverse*, *trigonometrical station*, *interior not examined* and *resurvey* are gone with
them, replaced by what they meant.

What is left is one image: **mapped ground, then a blank stretch labelled NOT MAPPED, then mapped
ground again.** Four stops instead of twelve. The four hazards are pins on the blank stretch with
no labels on the map at all — they are named once, in four words each, further down the page.

On a phone the map does not move: you get the whole thing in one wide strip, all three kinds of
ground at once. That turned out to be the clearest version of it, so it is what phones get.

## Recoloured

### The Long Light
You read the first frame as *standing on a long pier — will I jump*, and called the sequence
static and stale, reinforced by an unsmiling face.

The page is now white, and the film is a warm strip on it rather than being the whole dark page.
The page-wide grey vignette and the film grain have gone — between them they were most of the
gloom. The frames are re-graded warm and opened up about two stops. And the sequence is reversed:
it now opens on her face, looking straight at you, and ends on the wide open water rather than
beginning there.

### Second Horizon
Your note: *a night sky associates with getting darker as the night (years) progresses —
depressing, in this retirement context.* That is exactly right, and it was the wrong way round.

It now runs the other way. The page opens before dawn and ends in full morning, and once the sun
is properly up the whole document inverts to dark type on a light ground. Same mechanic, opposite
direction, opposite meaning. The land below the horizon is a lit violet instead of black.

The headline no longer says *sunset years*. It says: **The sun does not set. It comes up somewhere
else.**

### Unretired
*Monochrome dreary. Font size definitely in your face. Title does not gel with the remainder of
the information.* Three notes, three changes.

It is no longer monochrome — the light chapters carry a coral accent and the inverted chapters
are the brightened brand violet instead of black. The poster type is down by about a third, so it
is still emphatic but no longer shouting. And the title now has one line directly under it doing
the job you said was missing: *the word nobody uses for the part after the date. Preparing for it
is skills work, and it is teachable.*

### The Quiet Room
*Sombre and boring. Colours subdued and dreary — even the coffee is black.* And: *a quiet room
connotes with prayer.*

The aged cream and the paper-fibre texture are gone; the paper is now white. The photographs are
opened up. The single thread running down the page — which was the faintest thing on it — is now
the brightest, and it runs violet into coral as it descends.

### Third Act
You said you personally liked the black, white and red, and that the film-in-three-acts structure
appealed. You also said the mood does not fit the audience.

So the structure is untouched. What changed is the value of each of the three colours: the cream
is no longer aged, the black carries a violet cast rather than being flat, and the red is
brighter. And **Act III — the act about the work — now runs lit**, on a bright ground. The film no
longer plays from first frame to last in the dark.
